\subsection{Captured Contextual Data:}
To minimize participant burden and interaction costs, intervention architectures increasingly rely on passive sensing to establish contextual awareness.

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=1\textwidth]{sections/charts/bubbleChart.pdf}
\label{fig:bubble_matrix}
\end{figure}

As illustrated in the Context Mapping matrix (Figure~\ref{fig:bubble_matrix}), this contextual framework is organized around three core dimensions: What contextual variables are captured, How they are technologically acquired, and What For they are used, referring to their functional clinical objective. Across 291 mapped contextual instances, including 223 context acquisition mechanisms (How) and 68 intervention objectives (What For), the distribution centers primarily on software-level indicators. Computational Context accounts for 56.0\% of all instances (n = 163), followed by Physical Context (24.4\%, n = 71) and Social Context (19.6\%, n = 57).

Within the Computational Context domain (n = 163; 123 collection mechanisms and 40 intervention targets), In-App Telemetry constitutes the largest single subcategory, accounting for 60 data points (45 mechanisms and 15 targets; 36.8\% of the domain). This is followed by Micro-Temporal patterns with 39 data points (27 mechanisms, 12 targets; 23.9\%), Task Completion metrics with 36 data points (29 mechanisms, 7 targets; 22.1\%), and Macro-Timeline behavioral rhythms with 28 data points (22 mechanisms, 6 targets; 17.2\%).

Regarding Physical Context (n = 71, including 53 collection mechanisms and 18 intervention targets), research effort focuses on Mobility \& Movement data, which represents 32 data points (24 mechanisms and 8 targets; 45.1\% of the domain), primarily captured via GPS/Geofencing (n = 28) and Bluetooth sensors (n = 11). The remaining physical subcategories occupy smaller proportions: Semantic Context (14 data points; 9 mechanisms, 5 targets; 19.7\%), Absolute Geolocation (13 data points; 11 mechanisms, 2 targets; 18.3\%), and Ambient Environment data (12 data points; 9 mechanisms, 3 targets; 16.9\%).

In the Social Context domain (n = 57; 47 collection mechanisms and 10 intervention targets), Communication metrics predominate with 22 data points (17 mechanisms and 5 targets; 38.6\% of the domain). This is followed by Physical Proximity sensors (16 data points; 13 mechanisms, 3 targets; 28.1\%), Peer / Community signals (13 data points; 11 mechanisms, 2 targets; 22.8\%), and Clinical / Coach Support indicators (6 data points; 6 mechanisms, 0 targets; 10.5\%).

On the functional target side (What For), Adaptive Therapy represents the primary objective (28 data points; 41.2\%), followed by Healthcare Manager Support (25 data points; 36.8\%), Learning Algorithms (10 data points; 14.7\%), and general User Support (5 data points; 7.4\%). On the technical execution side, the How\ subsection {Intervention} (n = 84; 37.7\% of mechanisms) functions as the primary delivery backbone.

\subsection{Inerventions mechanisms and classification}
\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{sections/charts/mechanismClassificationStacked.pdf}
    \caption{Distribution of intervention mechanisms stacked by operational classification.}
    \label{fig:intervention_mechanisms_stacked}
\end{figure}

As evident from Figure~\ref{fig:intervention_mechanisms_stacked}, there is notable variation in both overall frequency and operational classification across the six identified intervention mechanisms (comprising 49 total co-occurrences). This classification describes three intervention delivery paradigms: Active interventions require direct, conscious engagement or decision-making from the user or clinician within the application; Passive interventions operate autonomously in the background without requiring user initiative; and Hybrid interventions pair automated background processes with human decision-making or collaborative workflows.

The primary mechanism across the examined corpus is Triggered delivery (n=15 co-occurrences), which relies on predefined rule-based logic to initiate interventions when specific contextual conditions are met. Triggered mechanisms display a distribution across all three operational types, led by Active implementations (n=6), followed by Passive (n=5) and Hybrid (n=4) configurations.

The second most common intervention mechanisms (n=9 co-occurrences each) are Algorithmic, Dashboard, and Manual interventions. Algorithmic interventions (n=9) are led by Active classifications (n=4), with substantial representation in Hybrid (n=3) and Passive (n=2) modes. Meanwhile, both Dashboard (n=9) and Manual (n=9) interventions exhibit a balanced split across Active (n=3), Hybrid (n=3), and Passive (n=3) classifications.

Occupying an intermediate tier, Escalated interventions (n=6 co-occurrences) also display an even distribution across Active (n=2), Hybrid (n=2), and Passive (n=2) classifications. Finally, User-driven interventions represent the least frequent mechanism (n=1 co-occurrence) and are exclusively operationalized as Active (100\%), relying entirely on participant initiative to launch modules or request assistance.

\subsection{Studies Designs and Evaluation Metrics}
\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{sections/charts/EMSTDheatmap.pdf}
    \caption{Distribution of study designs and evaluation metrics.}
    \label{fig:metrics_designs_heatmap}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:metrics_designs_heatmap} gives an overview of the co-occurrence matrix mapping study designs against evaluation metrics ($N = 42$ classified studies). Pilot \& Feasibility study designs represent the most prevalent methodology ($n = 20$; 47.6\%), followed by Experimental Randomized Controlled Trials ($n = 14$; 33.3\%), Formative \& Design studies ($n = 5$; 11.9\%), and Observational or Quasi-experimental studies ($n = 3$; 7.1\%).Across evaluation metrics, Acceptability \& Feasibility measures appear in 100.0\% of Pilot \& Feasibility studies ($n = 20$), 100.0\% of Observational studies ($n = 3$), 85.7\% of RCTs ($n = 12$), and 80.0\% of Formative \& Design studies ($n = 4$). In parallel, Clinical Efficacy \& Effectiveness is captured in 100.0\% of RCTs ($n = 14$) and Observational studies ($n = 3$), 80.0\% of Pilot \& Feasibility studies ($n = 16$), and 20.0\% of Formative studies ($n = 1$). Similarly, User Engagement \& Adherence is present in Observational studies ($n = 3$; 100.0\%), RCTs ($n = 12$; 85.7\%), and Pilot studies ($n = 15$; 75.0\%), while accounting for 20.0\% ($n = 1$) in initial Formative \& Design phases.Usability \& UX metrics are predominantly applied in Formative \& Design studies ($n = 4$; 80.0\%) and Pilot \& Feasibility studies ($n = 12$; 60.0\%), whereas their inclusion drops in RCTs ($n = 6$; 42.9\%) and is absent from Observational studies ($n = 0$; 0.0\%).

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Interpretation of Results}
% PRISMA Item 23a & 23b

\subsubsection{Evolution of the Field, Target Disorders, and Hardware Ecosystems}

The temporal trajectory of publications identified in this systematic review (Figure~\ref{fig:disorders_devices}) shows a marked acceleration beginning in 2020, after an extended period of low, sporadic output between 2016 and 2019 (1-5 studies annually). Research activity expanded substantially across 2020 ($n = 3$), 2021 ($n = 7$), 2023 ($n = 5$), and 2024 ($n = 7$), reaching its peak in 2025 ($n = 8$), with the early months of 2026 ($n = 2$) indicating continued momentum up to our search cutoff. Notably, no eligible studies were published in 2022. This overall trajectory mirrors macro-level shifts across digital health and psychiatric informatics: the acute disruptions and social restrictions precipitated by the COVID-19 pandemic catalyzed a global surge in psychological distress \cite{santomauro2021covid19depression, chekole2021covidmentalhealth} and generated unprecedented clinical demand for remote, scalable, contactless mental health interventions \cite{bufano2023digital}. The temporary publication gap observed in 2022 exemplifies the translational latency inherent to clinical research; early pandemic initiatives required multi-year cycles to navigate institutional review boards, establish sensor infrastructure, recruit psychiatric cohorts, and complete intervention trials before yielding peer-reviewed publications. Crucially, whereas early pandemic-era sensing concentrated primarily on retrospective, opportunistic behavioral observation \cite{sanudo2020covidactivity}, the subsequent wave of publications reflects a methodological transition from passive digital phenotyping toward active, context-aware therapeutic delivery \cite{vangenugten2025jitai, nahum2025just}.

Regarding diagnostic targets (Q1), the eligible studies addressed nine distinct DSM-5 mental health conditions and category groupings, yielding 50 disorder assignments across the 40 included studies. The evidence base is heavily concentrated on internalizing conditions, with research focusing predominantly on depressive disorders alone (18 studies; 45.0\%), followed by interventions addressing anxiety and depressive disorders concurrently (6 studies; 15.0\%). Consequently, depression represented a primary therapeutic target in three out of every five included investigations (24 studies; 60.0\%). This pronounced clinical focus aligns with scoping reviews of mobile sensing and digital phenotyping \cite{choi2024digitalphenotyping, busshart2026digitalphenotyping, dumas2026digitalphenotyping}, reflecting both epidemiological necessity and methodological tractability. Affective and anxiety symptoms correspond directly to validated, low-burden digital behavioral markers: depressive psychomotor retardation, social withdrawal, and circadian disruption manifest objectively in reduced GPS location entropy, extended homestay duration, altered sleep-wake architecture, and diminished communication dynamics \cite{saeb2015mobilephone, deangel2022passivemonitoring, shen2025passive}. Furthermore, evidence-based psychotherapeutic paradigms for depression and anxiety; most notably Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) and Behavioral Activation (BA); are exceptionally well-suited for modular decomposition into ecologically situated micro-interventions, such as momentary cognitive reframing prompts or activity-scheduling nudges delivered during opportune contextual windows \cite{balaskas2021ecological, leaning2024smartphone, kang2026behavioralactivation}.

Beyond depression and anxiety, other diagnostic domains were represented far less frequently. Sleep-wake disorders (4 studies; 10.0\%) \cite{werner-seidler2017sleepninja, pulantara2018insomnia, pulantara2018irest, philip2020kanopee} and feeding and eating disorders (3 studies; 7.5\%) \cite{young2021myfoodmood, kasson2024teensengagement, sanchezroman2025eatingdisorders} constituted the next largest categories. These conditions represent clinical domains where external contextual rhythms (such as bedtime routines, ambient sleep conditions, or meal-timing schedules) intersect directly with core symptom pathology, providing tangible anchors for just-in-time adaptation. In contrast, somatic symptom and related disorders (2 studies; 5.0\%), substance-related and addictive disorders (2 studies; 5.0\%) \cite{adams2021mhealthapp, heinz2024opioidusedisorder}, neurodevelopmental disorders (2 studies; 5.0\%) \cite{yang2023appcbt, spaargaren2025sam}, trauma- and stressor-related disorders (1 study; 2.5\%) \cite{davis2024renewptsd}, and suicidal ideation (1 study; 2.5\%) were minimally represented. The acute underrepresentation of high-acuity and trauma-related conditions underscores significant clinical, ethical, and algorithmic barriers \cite{martinezmartin2021ethical}. High-risk psychiatric conditions, such as active suicidal ideation or acute post-traumatic distress, entail substantial safety liabilities; relying on automated mobile sensing to detect acute crises introduces severe hazards of false-negative omissions \cite{adler2024actionablesensing}. Clinicians and ethics boards remain appropriately cautious about delegating high-stakes clinical management to mobile algorithms without fail-safe, human-supervised crisis escalation pathways. Moreover, complex conditions such as schizophrenia spectrum disorders \cite{kidd2019app4independence, ben-zeev2021core, kidd2025appindependence} present non-linear, multi-dimensional symptomatology that is difficult to model through consumer sensors without intensive clinical partnership \cite{lanata2015complexity, hassan2025utility}.

Analysis of hardware platforms (Q2; Figure~\ref{fig:disorders_devices}) demonstrates 52 platform assignments across the 40 studies, exposing a striking architectural divergence between sensing-only research and actual intervention delivery. Mobile phones dominated the technological ecosystem entirely, deployed in all 40 studies (100.0\%) and serving as the exclusive hardware interface in 30 studies (75.0\%). Supplementary consumer technologies accounted for only a minor share: tablets were used in six studies (15.0\%), wrist-worn wearables in four studies (10.0\%), and positioning sensors (e.g., Bluetooth Low Energy beacons) in two studies (5.0\%). In the ten studies (25.0\%) that deployed multi-device configurations, secondary hardware always complemented, rather than substituted for, the smartphone: eight studies paired smartphones with a single auxiliary device (tablets: 5, 12.5\%; wearables: 2, 5.0\%; positioning sensors: 1, 2.5\%), while two studies incorporated two auxiliary devices (combining tablets with wearables, or wearables with positioning sensors; 2.5\% each). 

This distribution presents a sharp contrast to broader passive-sensing surveys \cite{ometov2021, sheikh2021passivesensing, hickey2021wearablemental}, where smartwatches and wristbands figure prominently for continuous autonomic monitoring (e.g., heart rate variability, electrodermal activity, and skin temperature). In context-aware interventions, however, wearables occupy a distinctly peripheral role. This divergence stems from fundamental ergonomic and technical trade-offs. Delivering multi-step cognitive and behavioral interventions—such as interactive thought records, behavioral activation diaries, and video-based psychoeducation—requires substantial screen real estate and flexible interactive input modalities that cannot be accommodated on miniature smartwatch displays. Furthermore, continuous background sensing on wrist-worn consumer wearables imposes rapid battery depletion, encounters aggressive operating system background execution limits, relies on fragile wireless tethering pipelines, and experiences steep participant non-adherence over multi-week protocols \cite{ometov2021, hassan2025utility, jung2025key}. Tablets occupy a specialized clinical niche, utilized primarily in clinician-facilitated sessions, maternal-infant dyads \cite{joyce2026beam}, or pediatric cohorts requiring larger visual interfaces. Finally, standalone positioning sensors remain restricted to bespoke experimental trials due to procurement costs, deployment overhead, and the absence of consumer-grade ubiquity.

\subsubsection{Contextual Telemetry: Balancing Granularity, OS Policies, and Privacy}

The Context Mapping matrix (Figure~\ref{fig:bubble_matrix}) articulates how external contextual dimensions are acquired and operationalized within mobile interventions across 379 mapped contextual instances: 205 context-capturing mechanisms (\textit{How}) and 174 intervention purposes (\textit{What For}). A primary finding of this review is the overwhelming predominance of computing and temporal context over physical and social dimensions. Computing and temporal context accounted for 222 of 379 instances (58.6\%; 117 capture techniques and 105 intervention purposes) and was incorporated in 38 of the 40 included studies (95.0\%). In comparison, social context (79 instances, 20.8\%; 13 studies, 32.5\%) and physical context (78 instances, 20.6\%; 11 studies, 27.5\%) each constituted approximately one-fifth of the identified instances.

Within the computing and temporal domain (Q3), in-app telemetry represented the single largest subcategory (82 instances; 36.9\%; deployed across 26 studies, 65.0\%), followed by task completion metrics (53 instances; 23.9\%; 17 studies, 42.5\%), micro-temporal patterns (45 instances; 20.3\%; 12 studies), and macro-temporal timeline metrics (42 instances; 18.9\%; 12 studies). This heavy concentration reflects a pragmatic convergence of technical accessibility, operating system constraints, and ecological feasibility. Over the past five years, major mobile platform vendors (Google Android and Apple iOS) have enacted increasingly stringent background privacy and energy-conservation frameworks. Aggressive background process termination (e.g., Android Doze mode and foreground service restrictions in Android 12 through 14) and recurring runtime permission prompts for continuous background location (e.g., iOS CoreLocation tracking prompts and App Tracking Transparency) have made round-the-clock hardware sensor harvesting technically fragile, prone to substantial data loss, and battery-prohibitive \cite{dumas2026digitalphenotyping, lee2023digitalphenotyping}. In contrast, in-app telemetry and temporal metrics (e.g., session durations, screen-view sequences, module completion cadence, timestamps, and day-of-week rhythms) execute natively within the application container. They require zero invasive system permissions, impose negligible energy drain, and operate with minimal latency via device-side automation (which accounted for 78 of the 205 capture instances; 38.0\%). These indicators directly operationalize treatment adherence, engagement decay, and intervention dosage without subjecting users to continuous surveillance.

In the physical context domain (78 instances; 11 studies), research effort centered predominantly on mobility and movement metrics (36 instances; 46.2\%; 7 studies), whereas semantic context (17 instances; 21.8\%; 4 studies), ambient environment (15 instances; 19.2\%; 3 studies), and absolute geolocation (10 instances; 12.8\%; 2 studies) were used far more selectively. The prioritization of abstracted mobility features (e.g., location variance, normalized entropy, homestay duration, and travel radius) over raw geospatial coordinates aligns with foundational digital phenotyping research validating spatial exploration as an objective proxy for depressive withdrawal and psychomotor disturbance \cite{saeb2015mobilephone, onnela2016digitalphenotyping, shen2025passive}. Furthermore, abstracted movement patterns protect participant privacy, whereas raw latitude and longitude coordinates provide little immediate psychotherapeutic utility while generating acute surveillance concerns. Similarly, ambient environmental metrics (e.g., ambient sound and illuminance) and semantic contexts are constrained by severe privacy liabilities. Continuous acoustic sensing introduces pervasive eavesdropping concerns among clinical participants \cite{martinezmartin2021ethical, sheikh2021passivesensing}, directly explaining why microphone and audio recording accounted for only 8 capture instances (3.9\%).

In the social context domain (79 instances; 13 studies), communication metrics represented the largest subcategory (30 instances; 38.0\%; 6 studies), followed by peer and community signals (20 instances; 25.3\%; 4 studies), physical proximity sensors (16 instances; 20.3\%; 3 studies), and clinical or coach support indicators (13 instances; 16.5\%; 3 studies). Communication logs (e.g., text and call volume metadata) and in-app peer interactions provide objective, non-intrusive windows into socialization rhythms and social fragmentation \cite{langener2023capturing}. Notably, clinical and coach support metrics functioned exclusively as structured collaborative touchpoints and adherence indicators rather than autonomous algorithmic tailoring targets. This boundary reflects an essential clinical governance standard: human therapeutic alliance and professional crisis management are maintained through structured direct contact and clinician supervision, rather than being delegated to unsupervised automated systems \cite{rogan2024health, adler2024actionablesensing}.

Regarding context-capturing techniques (Q4; 205 instances), passive techniques operating autonomously in the background accounted for 176 instances (85.9\%), led by device-side automation (78; 38.0\%), GPS/geofencing (24; 11.7\%), in-app tracking and telemetry (24; 11.7\%), and online server usage tracking (18; 8.8\%). Active capture techniques requiring direct user input represented 29 instances (14.1\%), comprising self-report (18; 8.8\%) and direct contact or prompts (11; 5.4\%). This distribution demonstrates that mobile interventions strive to minimize participant reporting burden by harvesting native software and sensor streams. 

On the functional target side (Q5; 174 instances), external context was leveraged overwhelmingly for user support (99 instances; 56.9\%), followed by adaptive therapy (40; 23.0\%) and healthcare provider support (29; 16.7\%), whereas learning algorithms accounted for only 6 instances (3.4\%). Contextual information is thus utilized primarily to deliver timely psychoeducation, provide self-management tools, and generate personalized feedback directly to the user (79.9\% combined for user support and adaptive therapy), or to populate clinical dashboards for care teams (16.7\%). In sharp contrast, autonomous learning algorithms (e.g., machine learning models for risk prediction or reinforcement learning) remain exceedingly rare in deployed interventions. This highlights a pervasive translation gap: despite substantial theoretical enthusiasm for AI-driven adaptive interventions, clinical researchers and developers avoid uninterpretable, black-box algorithms due to safety regulations, clinical liability, and the necessity of predictable, rule-based therapeutic delivery \cite{adler2024actionablesensing, teles2017situationawareness}.

\subsubsection{Intervention Architectures: User-Driven Agency, Contextual Triggers, and Human-in-the-Loop Oversight}

Our synthesis of intervention delivery mechanisms and operational paradigms (Q6; Figures~\ref{fig:intervention_mechanisms_stacked} and \ref{fig:intervention_mechanisms_stacked2}) provides crucial empirical insight into the architectural mechanics of context-aware digital mental health. Across 117 identified mechanism--paradigm co-occurrences, active delivery predominated (69 co-occurrences; 59.0\%), followed by hybrid delivery (33; 28.2\%) and passive delivery (15; 12.8\%). Among specific mechanisms, user-driven delivery—in which participants themselves launch modules, practice exercises, or initiate support—was by far the most frequent configuration (45 co-occurrences; 38.5\%: 34 active, 7 hybrid, 4 passive). It was followed by triggered delivery (24; 20.5\%: 14 active, 6 hybrid, 4 passive), manual delivery (21; 17.9\%: 11 active, 7 hybrid, 3 passive), dashboard-based delivery (14; 12.0\%: 5 active, 7 hybrid, 2 passive), algorithmic delivery (9; 7.7\%: 4 active, 4 hybrid, 1 passive), and escalated delivery mechanisms (4; 3.4\%: 1 active, 2 hybrid, 1 passive).

The strong predominance of user-driven mechanisms (38.5\%) and active delivery paradigms (59.0\%) provides a vital corrective to early ubiquitous computing assumptions, which envisioned mobile mental health systems operating silently and autonomously in the background without user initiative. In evidence-based psychotherapy, therapeutic behavior change fundamentally requires active cognitive elaboration, conscious reflection, and deliberate skill rehearsal. Mental health interventions cannot be passively consumed or administered in the manner of pharmacological treatments; patients must actively engage in cognitive restructuring, exposure exercises, or behavioral activation \cite{balaskas2021ecological, leaning2024smartphone}. Moreover, fully autonomous background interventions risk inducing psychological reactance, cognitive dissonance, or alert fatigue if users feel surveilled or prompted inappropriately. Consequently, context awareness does not supplant user agency; rather, it scaffolds and empowers it. Contextual intelligence identifies opportune moments of receptivity, prepares tailored content, and surfaces situated nudges, while preserving patient autonomy over when and how to engage.

Triggered delivery (20.5\%) functions as the indispensable operational bridge of Just-In-Time Adaptive Interventions (JITAIs) \cite{nahumshani2018jitai, nahum2025just}. By deploying predefined deterministic rules, triggered systems link objective contextual states (such as prolonged home stay, elevated ambient noise, or app inactivity streaks) directly to targeted therapeutic prompts. While recent systematic reviews have highlighted that mental health JITAIs have historically relied almost entirely on self-reported ecological momentary assessments for intervention tailoring \cite{vangenugten2025jitai, van2025beyond, hsu2025personalized}, our findings establish that when external contextual streams are incorporated, deterministic rule-based triggers provide an interpretable, robust link between passive sensing and actionable clinical micro-interventions.

The distribution of algorithmic mechanisms (7.7\% of delivery mechanisms and 3.4\% of functional purposes) illustrates the cautious, nascent adoption of machine learning in active treatment delivery. Notably, algorithmic configurations were divided equally between active ($n = 4$) and hybrid ($n = 4$) paradigms, with only a single purely passive implementation. This reflects widespread reluctance among clinical investigators to grant machine learning models full autonomous control over psychological interventions without user confirmation or clinical oversight \cite{adler2024actionablesensing, rogan2024health}. Instead, algorithmic outputs are channeled into decision-support frameworks that maintain human agency.

This imperative for clinical governance is further reinforced by the prominent representation of dashboard-mediated (12.0\%), manual (17.9\%), and escalated delivery mechanisms (3.4\%), which belong predominantly to the hybrid paradigm. Dashboards synthesize raw sensor telemetry and adherence trajectories into actionable clinical summaries, bridging automated mobile data with multidisciplinary care teams \cite{dowding2015dashboards, bosch2025integration}. Manual mechanisms preserve therapeutic alliance by enabling clinicians or peers to provide individualized guidance. Finally, escalation mechanisms embody an indispensable safety protocol in digital psychiatry: passive continuous monitoring runs in the background until an acute risk threshold is breached (such as sustained severe insomnia, profound social withdrawal, or crisis survey responses), immediately activating client safety plans or notifying supervisory clinicians \cite{martinezmartin2021ethical}.

Concrete exemplar architectures identified across our synthesized corpus illustrate these multi-faceted delivery paradigms:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Multimodal Sensing and Collaborative Care:} Van Heerden et al.~\cite{vanheerden2024standstrong} (StandStrong) combined passive mobile and wearable sensing (mobility, ambient light, physical proximity) with community-based peer and nurse coaching for maternal depression, demonstrating how hybrid workflows anchor automated sensing in supportive human relationships.
    \item \textbf{Personal Sensing in Primary Care Integration:} Stiles-Shields et al.~\cite{stiles-shields2024personalsensing} evaluated personal sensing (GPS mobility and communication metadata) paired with primary care clinician collaboration, showing how contextual telemetry enriches clinical consultations without overwhelming patients.
    \item \textbf{Reinforcement Learning in JITAIs:} Aguilera et al.~\cite{aguilera2024diamante} (DIAMANTE trial) operationalized reinforcement learning algorithms and micro-temporal tracking to deliver dynamically tailored physical activity text messages targeting depressive symptoms, illustrating the potential of adaptive learning when coupled with user-facing prompts.
    \item \textbf{Peer-Supported Telemetry and Serious Mental Illness:} Blonigen et al.~\cite{blonigen2023peersupported} and Kidd et al.~\cite{kidd2019app4independence, kidd2025appindependence} (App4Independence / A4i) deployed multi-component platforms pairing in-app telemetry, peer discussion forums, and clinician portals for veterans and individuals with schizophrenia spectrum disorders, establishing vital relational buffers that mitigate digital attrition in high-vulnerability populations.
    \item \textbf{Transdiagnostic Contextual Micro-Interventions:} Bell et al.~\cite{bell2023mello} (Mello app) leveraged semantic context, telemetry, and task completion metrics to trigger momentary ecological interventions targeting repetitive negative thinking in young people with depression and anxiety.
    \item \textbf{Temporal Adaptation in Behavioral Sleep Medicine:} Pulantara et al.~\cite{pulantara2018irest, pulantara2018insomnia} (iREST) demonstrated context-driven JITAI architectures that combine micro-temporal scheduling, sleep tracking, and coach-mediated feedback to treat behavioral insomnia.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Translational Trajectory: The Pilot Treadmill and Methodological Gaps}

The cross-tabulation of study designs and evaluation dimensions (Q7; Figure~\ref{fig:metrics_designs_heatmap}) provides a clear diagnostic of the methodological maturity and translational bottlenecks characterizing context-aware mental health interventions. Across the 40 classified studies, pilot and feasibility studies were the most prevalent design (18 studies; 45.0\%), followed by randomized controlled trials (RCTs; 14 studies; 35.0\%), formative and design studies (5 studies; 12.5\%), and observational or quasi-experimental studies (3 studies; 7.5\%).

The heavy concentration of research within pilot and feasibility frameworks illustrates what implementation scientists describe as the ``pilot treadmill'' or the digital translation chasm in behavioral healthcare \cite{skivington2021complexinterventions, bosch2025integration}. Deploying context-aware digital interventions requires continuous mobile sensor harvesting, background OS compatibility management, real-time feature engineering, robust cloud infrastructure, and complex clinical risk protocols. Consequently, investigators must invest substantial funding and effort into establishing basic technical feasibility, battery optimization, and protocol acceptability before advancing to full-scale clinical trials. Furthermore, the rapid obsolescence cycle of mobile operating systems and hardware platforms frequently outpaces the multi-year timeline required to secure competitive grant funding, obtain institutional ethics approvals, recruit psychiatric cohorts, and execute definitive phase-III trials \cite{miralles2020smartphone, leaning2024smartphone}. Nevertheless, the presence of 14 randomized controlled trials (35.0\%) indicates that the field is progressively transitioning beyond purely descriptive feasibility work toward rigorous confirmatory evidence.

Regarding evaluation dimensions, acceptability and feasibility was evaluated in 37 of the 40 studies (92.5\%), including all pilot and feasibility studies (18/18; 100.0\%) and observational studies (3/3; 100.0\%), 12 of 14 RCTs (85.7\%), and 4 of 5 formative studies (80.0\%). Clinical efficacy and effectiveness was assessed in 33 studies (82.5\%), evaluated in all RCTs (14/14; 100.0\%) and observational studies (3/3; 100.0\%), 15 of 18 pilot studies (83.3\%), and 1 formative study (20.0\%). User engagement and adherence was assessed in 29 studies (72.5\%), encompassing all observational studies (3/3; 100.0\%), 12 RCTs (85.7\%), 13 pilot studies (72.2\%), and 1 formative study (20.0\%). This consistent focus on engagement metrics reflects widespread recognition that digital mental health interventions suffer from steep engagement decay and high participant attrition over time \cite{stone2023evaluation, kraft2024mobile}. While passive sensing alleviates the burden of frequent self-reporting, sustaining participant engagement with triggered therapeutic recommendations remains a major challenge.

Crucially, usability and user experience (UX) metrics exhibited a pronounced, concerning decline as studies advanced along the translational continuum: usability was assessed in 4 of 5 formative and design studies (80.0\%) and 12 of 18 pilot and feasibility studies (66.7\%), but dropped to only 6 of 14 RCTs (42.9\%) and was completely absent from observational studies (0/3; 0.0\%). This inverse trajectory exposes a major methodological blind spot in digital psychiatry. As interventions transition to formal clinical efficacy evaluations, researchers frequently treat software interfaces and interaction designs as static, finalized artifacts, deprioritizing user-centered evaluation. Yet interaction friction, intrusive notification cadences, cognitive load, and battery drain are primary drivers of non-adherence and trial dropout in digital therapeutics \cite{rogan2024health, stone2023evaluation}. When an RCT demonstrates diminished clinical efficacy, failing to evaluate usability makes it impossible to disentangle whether the therapeutic mechanism itself was ineffective or whether flawed interaction design and notification fatigue caused silent participant disengagement. Sustaining clinical efficacy in real-life deployment requires that usability and user experience remain continuous, primary endpoints throughout mature confirmatory trials.

\subsection{Limitations of the Evidence}
% PRISMA Item 23c

The body of empirical evidence synthesized in this systematic review is subject to several inherent clinical, technological, and methodological limitations:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.}]
    \item \textbf{Clinical and Intervention Heterogeneity:} The included studies spanned nine distinct diagnostic categories, ranging from subclinical depressive symptoms to major depressive disorder, generalized anxiety disorder, eating disorders, and schizophrenia spectrum disorders. Concurrently, the deployed therapeutic modalities varied considerably, encompassing cognitive behavioral therapy, behavioral activation, mindfulness, dialectical behavior skills, and peer-supported collaborative care. This substantial clinical and theoretical heterogeneity precluded quantitative meta-analytic pooling and complicates direct comparisons regarding the relative therapeutic efficacy of specific external context triggers.
    
    \item \textbf{Preponderance of Short-Term, Underpowered Pilot Trials:} Nearly half of the evidence base (45.0\%) consists of pilot and feasibility trials, which are frequently characterized by small sample sizes and brief intervention windows ranging from two to eight weeks. Such brief observation periods are insufficient to determine long-term clinical efficacy, symptom relapse prevention, longitudinal sensor adherence, or habituation to automated notifications over time.
    
    \item \textbf{Lack of Standardized Sensing and Feature Protocols:} The field exhibits a pronounced lack of standardized sensing protocols, sampling frequencies, and data preprocessing pipelines. For example, physical mobility was variously operationalized across studies via raw coordinates, proprietary geofencing boundaries, normalized location entropy, or coarse step counts, with diverse temporal epoch aggregations. This methodological fragmentation hampers cross-study reproducibility, impedes algorithmic benchmark comparisons, and limits the generalizability of predictive models \cite{leaning2024smartphone, deangel2022passivemonitoring}.
    
    \item \textbf{Algorithmic Opacity and Scarcity of Machine Learning Adaptations:} The vast majority of intervention architectures rely on rigid, hand-crafted deterministic rules, while adaptive machine learning models accounted for only 3.4\% of functional purposes and 7.7\% of delivery mechanisms. Where algorithmic predictions are utilized, the frequent reliance on opaque, black-box models creates understandable hesitation among clinicians, who remain legally and ethically accountable for patient care yet cannot audit the underlying decision logic \cite{rogan2024health, adler2024actionablesensing}.
    
    \item \textbf{Demographic, Socioeconomic, and Platform Selection Biases:} The majority of included trials recruited predominantly young, tech-literate, urban participants, often drawn from university student pools or online volunteer cohorts. Socioeconomically disadvantaged populations, older adults, and individuals with severe functional or cognitive impairments remain markedly underrepresented. Furthermore, systematic hardware disparities—such as differential background sensing restrictions across premium operating systems versus budget smartphones with aggressive vendor-specific battery managers—introduce platform-level biases that distort data completeness and intervention delivery across socioeconomic strata.
    
    \item \textbf{Absence of Formal Risk of Bias and Evidence Certainty Assessments:} In accordance with our methodological protocol (Section~\ref{sec:methods:data-items}), no formal assessment of study risk of bias (e.g., Cochrane RoB 2 or ROBINS-I) or certainty of evidence (GRADE) was performed, because this review aimed to characterize how external context is acquired and used across diverse study designs rather than to compute pooled effect estimates. While appropriate for a descriptive architectural synthesis, readers must interpret reported clinical efficacy outcomes with caution.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Limitations of the Review Process}
% PRISMA Item 23d

Several methodological constraints intrinsic to the systematic review process must also be acknowledged:

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{\arabic*.}]
    \item \textbf{Database Coverage and Language Criteria:} While our search strategy encompassed four major multidisciplinary and biomedical databases (Scopus, Web of Science Core Collection, APA PsycInfo, and PubMed) without publication date limits up to February 5, 2026, the search was explicitly restricted to English-language peer-reviewed articles and conference proceedings. Relevant investigations published in other languages or indexed exclusively in regional or specialized engineering repositories may have been omitted.
    
    \item \textbf{Exclusion of Grey Literature and Commercial App Store Offerings:} In strict adherence to PRISMA standards for peer-reviewed evidence, our eligibility criteria excluded non-peer-reviewed grey literature, preprints, patents, and commercial mobile application repositories (such as the Apple App Store and Google Play). Numerous commercial mental health applications currently incorporate context-aware push notifications and device-usage tracking; however, because their algorithmic architectures and clinical efficacy outcomes are rarely published in peer-reviewed scientific literature, their technical implementations could not be evaluated.
    
    \item \textbf{Scope Boundary on External versus Internal Context:} To address the distinct situational ``why'' gap in ubiquitous computing, our eligibility criteria deliberately restricted inclusion to studies that captured and integrated external contextual information (physical, social, and computing/temporal factors), excluding interventions relying exclusively on internal physiological tracking (such as standalone heart rate variability or electrodermal activity biofeedback) or pure self-report symptom rating scales without external context. While this restriction was necessary to answer our core research questions, it intentionally set aside valuable literature on standalone autonomic biofeedback.
    
    \item \textbf{Publication and Positive Outcome Bias:} In digital health and mobile sensing, there exists a well-documented publication bias favoring positive feasibility, acceptability, and efficacy outcomes. Studies encountering severe technical failures—such as fatal application crashes, massive sensor data loss, unsustainable battery drain, or severe participant dropout—or null clinical outcomes are substantially less likely to be submitted or accepted for publication, potentially skewing the literature toward an overly optimistic appraisal of system reliability.
    
    \item \textbf{Technological Turnover and Publication Latency:} The rapid pace of consumer smart hardware and mobile operating system development outstrips academic publication cycles. The multi-year lag between study design, grant procurement, ethical approval, participant recruitment, trial completion, and peer-reviewed publication means that some reviewed architectures reflect operating system APIs and hardware specifications that have since been modified or deprecated by platform vendors.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
\textbf{Background:}
Ubiquitous computing underpins Ecological Momentary Interventions (EMIs) and Just-in-Time Adaptive Interventions (JITAIs) for mental health. However, this paradigm emphasizes internal physiological sensing (symptom tracking) over external contextual factors (situational causes), even though these factors are directly correlated with the mental issue.\newline

\noindent
\textbf{Objectives:}
This study systematically reviews, categorizes, and analyzes how physical, social, and computing/temporal contexts are integrated into consumer wearable and smartphone-based mental health interventions.\newline

\noindent
\textbf{Eligibility Criteria:}
Peer-reviewed articles from journals and conferences that evaluated mental health interventions using consumer-grade mobile devices and integrating external contextual data were included. Studies that relied on specialized non-consumer devices or exclusively tracked internal physiological metrics without external contextual integration were excluded.\newline

\noindent
\textbf{Sources:}
We conducted a comprehensive search across Scopus, PsycINFO, PubMed, and Web of Science.\newline

\noindent
\textbf{Selection Methods:}
Following PRISMA guidelines, titles and abstracts were screened using an automated machine-learning tool (ASReview), followed by independent full-text reviews. Data were extracted using a standardized framework covering physical, social, and computing and temporal external context.\newline

\noindent
\textbf{Results:}
40 studies met the final inclusion criteria. Computing and temporal context predominated (38 studies; 95.0\%), and external context was used mainly for user support (99 of 174 context-purpose instances; 56.9\%), followed by adaptive therapy (40; 23.0\%).\newline
\newline

\noindent
\textbf{Conclusions:}
The findings highlight a shift from passive symptom monitoring toward situation-aware intervention models, while also showing that context is used more often for direct user support than for adaptive or learning-based delivery. Bridging the existing ``why'' gap in ubiquitous computing will require multimodal external sensing and robust, privacy-preserving computational systems.\newline

\end{abstract}
